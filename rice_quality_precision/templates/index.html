📘 Grain Science Knowledge Base

Rice Agronomy, Cooking & Safety

Key facts on grain biology, aroma compounds, parboiling, storage best practices, and food safety standards.

Morphology

Grain Length & Aspect Ratio

Long grain is $\ge 3\times$ longer than wide (Basmati, Jasmine, IR64); medium grain is 2–3$\times$; short grain is plump and round (Sushi, Arborio, Bomba). Ratio determines cooking fluffiness versus stickiness.

Chemistry

Aromatic Rice Chemistry

Basmati, Jasmine, and Pandanwangi naturally contain high concentrations of 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline (2-AP), imparting their distinctive pandan and popcorn aroma.

Starch Science

Amylose vs Amylopectin

Standard long grain contains 20–25% amylose (grains stay separate). Glutinous (sticky) rice contains nearly 0% amylose and 100% amylopectin, causing it to stick when steamed.

Processing

Parboiled (Sella) Rice

Paddy is soaked, pressurized-steamed, and dried prior to milling. This drives B vitamins from the bran into the starchy kernel, hardens the grain, and minimizes cooking breakage.

Nutrition

Pigmented: Brown, Red & Black

Pigmented varieties retain the outer bran layer rich in dietary fibre, minerals, and anthocyanin antioxidants. The lipids in the germ require airtight, cool storage to prevent rancidity.

Milling

Broken Rice (Head Yield)

Kernels shorter than 3/4 of a whole grain are classified as brokens. While lower in market grade, clean broken rice is culinary staple for congee, Vietnamese Cơm tấm, and rice noodles.

Safety

Spoilage Signs & Aflatoxins

Musty or sour odors, damp clumping, or green/black patches signal fungal contamination (e.g. Aspergillus flavus), which can produce carcinogenic aflatoxins. Never wash and consume mouldy grain.

Food Safety

Cooked Rice & Bacillus cereus

Uncooked rice spores can survive boiling. Cool cooked rice rapidly within 1 hour, refrigerate below 5°C, and reheat to steaming hot ($\ge 75^\circ$C). Never leave at room temperature overnight.

Agronomy

Over 40,000 Rice Cultivars

The global germplasm repository holds tens of thousands of rice varieties adapted to dry uplands, deep wetlands, and high altitudes. The World Library can be extended via data/more_rice.csv.
